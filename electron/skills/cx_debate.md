# Policy / CX Debate Guide

> **Assume expertise.** The user is an experienced competitive debater and already knows the basics below (stock issues, flowing, what a DA/CP/K is, etc). Do not explain foundational concepts or define jargon unless the user explicitly asks you to break down a specific part. Use this skill for quick structural reference and — especially — the topic-specific argument content in the "This Year's Topic" section, which is the part most worth pulling from unprompted.

## Overview
Two-on-two. Aff defends a plan under a year-long resolution. Neg opposes. Same resolution all season. Aff has burden of proof — if tied, Neg wins via presumption (status quo).

| 2AC | 8 min | 9 min | Must answer everything |

| 2NC | 8 min | 9 min | Develops chosen Neg args |
| CX | 3 min | 3 min | Aff questions Neg |
| 1NR | 5 min | 6 min | Covers what 2NC didn't |
| 1AR | 5 min | 6 min | Must answer entire neg block — hardest speech |
| 2NR | 5 min | 6 min | Collapse to 1–2 args; frame the round |

**Negative block:** 2NC + 1NR = 13 consecutive minutes (HS) or 15 (college) of Neg speaking time.
**Prep time:** 8 min/team (HS), 10 min/team (college). Used between speeches at the team's discretion.

## Aff Burdens (Stock Issues)
- **Inherency** — the problem exists and the status quo is not fixing it
- **Harms / Advantages** — significant negative consequences in the SQ that the plan addresses
- **Solvency** — the plan actually fixes or reduces those harms
- **Topicality** — the plan falls within the bounds of the resolution

## Neg Toolkit — Deep Reference

### Disadvantages (DAs)
Plan causes harmful consequences that outweigh the advantages.
- **Uniqueness** — the harm doesn't exist (or is trending away) in the status quo
- **Link** — the plan triggers the harm
- **Internal link** — the chain connecting the link to the impact
- **Impact** — the harm itself (magnitude, probability, timeframe)
- To beat a DA: turn it, no-link it, outweigh on impact calculus, or take out uniqueness

**Common DA families (topic-agnostic):**
- **Politics DA** — plan costs/spends political capital, derails an unrelated bill, or triggers partisan backlash that changes an election/legislative outcome
- **Economy DA** — plan disrupts a market, industry, or fiscal indicator (jobs, stocks, growth, inflation)
- **Spending / Deficit DA** — plan's cost increases the deficit, crowds out other spending, or triggers austerity/backlash
- **Federalism DA** — federal action erodes state authority/autonomy, setting a precedent that guts state-level policy experimentation
- **Court DA / Backlash DA** — plan triggers litigation, a Supreme Court fight, or erodes judicial legitimacy
- **Relations DA** — plan affects an international relationship, alliance, or negotiation
- **Elections DA** — a policy-politics hybrid: plan becomes an election issue, shifting outcomes and downstream policy

### Counterplans (CPs)
Alternative policy that solves the case (in whole or part) without doing the plan as written, or doing it differently.
- Must be **competitive**: mutually exclusive with the plan, or net beneficial relative to doing both (the perm)
- **Permutation test**: Aff argues "perm — do both" (or "do the CP") to test whether the CP is truly competitive
- **Net benefit**: the DA or advantage that the CP solves/avoids but the plan doesn't — this is *why* the CP is a reason to reject the Aff, not just an alternative

**Common CP types:**
- **States CP** — the 50 states (and DC) do the plan instead of the federal government; competes off federalism/uniformity DAs, or the resolution's "federal government" actor wording
- **Agent CP** — a different actor within the federal government (an agency like HHS/CMS, the courts, executive order vs. legislation) does the plan
- **Process CP** — plan passes through a different legislative process (e.g., budget reconciliation instead of standalone bill, different vote threshold) to dodge a link (like a filibuster or bipartisanship DA)
- **Consult CP** — the USFG consults an actor (an ally, a domestic stakeholder, an international body) before acting; competes via the net benefit of "consultation" itself (relations, legitimacy)
- **PIC (Plan-Inclusive Counterplan)** — does everything the plan does *except* one component, isolating that component as the net benefit (e.g., "do the plan but exclude undocumented immigrants" / "exclude a specific benefit category")
- **Advantage CP** — solves the Aff's stated advantage(s) through a completely different mechanism, without needing to link to the Aff's disad-generating mechanism at all
- **International Fiat CP** — another country or international body acts instead
- **Conditions CP** — Aff's plan happens, but only if a condition is met (funding source, phase-in, sunset)

### Kritiks (Ks)
The Aff's assumptions, discourse, or representations are flawed and should be rejected independent of (or prior to) the plan's policy merits.
- **Link** — how the Aff's discourse/assumptions/method triggers the K
- **Impact** — the harm from those assumptions (often framed as prior to or a precondition for policy analysis)
- **Alternative** — what the Neg advocates instead (reject the aff, embrace a different epistemology/ethic, a specific praxis)
- **Framework** — the Neg's argument for *why* the K should be evaluated before/instead of the plan's consequences (fiat is illusory, representations matter first, etc.)

**Common K literature bases:** capitalism, biopower/Foucault, security, anthropocentrism, militarism, settler colonialism, afropessimism, disability studies, neoliberalism, necropolitics, structural racism/critical race theory

### Topicality (T)
The Aff's plan does not fall within the bounds of the resolution → jurisdictional, reject regardless of plan merits.
- **Violation** — what the Aff did wrong relative to the resolution's wording
- **Standards** — why your interpretation is better (limits, ground, predictability, bright-line)
- **Voting issue** — why T should be a voter (jurisdiction, fairness, education)

### Theory
Procedural fairness/education objections to how the debate itself is being conducted (distinct from T, which is about the resolution's wording).
- Common shells: **conditionality bad** (Neg can't kick contradictory advocacies), **multiple conditional worlds bad**, **PICs bad**, **floating PIKs bad**, **consult CPs bad** (illegitimate/artificially competitive), **states CP theory** (50-state fiat is unpredictable/unreal-world)
- Components: interpretation, violation, standards, voting issue — structurally identical to T but aimed at in-round practice rather than resolutional wording

## This Year's Topic (2026–27): National Health Insurance

**Resolution:** *Resolved: The United States federal government should establish national health insurance in the United States.*

Chosen by NSDA ballot (health insurance beat out a nuclear weapons topic area, 23–8). The last time a national health insurance resolution was debated at this level was over 30 years ago, so most current strategy is being built fresh rather than recycled from an existing topic file.

### Common Affirmative Mechanisms
- **Medicare for All** — single-payer, eliminates private insurance, built on/expanded from the existing Medicare model
- **Single-payer replacing private insurance (non-Medicare model)** — same end state as M4A but not necessarily built on Medicare's existing structure/rules
- **Revise/expand the ACA** — strengthens the 2010 Affordable Care Act: restoring or expanding federal subsidies, strengthening or reinstating the individual mandate, closing the Medicaid gap
- Advantage areas Affs commonly read: uninsurance/underinsurance harms, medical bankruptcy, health outcome disparities, administrative cost/bureaucracy waste from a multi-payer system, public health/pandemic preparedness

### Negative Ground on This Topic
- **Rationing / wait-times DA** — expanded government provision of insurance increases demand without proportionally increasing supply (doctors, facilities), producing rationing and longer wait times (parallel literature: NHS/Canada/single-payer systems abroad)
- **Private insurance industry disruption DA** — eliminating or shrinking private insurance disrupts a major existing industry: job losses in insurance administration, market/stock disruption, downstream economic effects
- **Politics/Spending DA** — national health insurance is enormously expensive and politically fraught; a plan this large is a prime link for a generic politics or spending DA (budget reconciliation fights, bipartisanship costs, election-year backlash)
- **Federalism DA / States CP** — states currently administer significant parts of health policy (Medicaid, insurance marketplaces, licensing); federalizing insurance is a strong link for a federalism DA and a natural fit for a **States CP** that lets states opt into varying models instead of one federal mandate
- **Quality/innovation DA** — argument that private-market competition drives medical innovation/R&D, and single-payer models reduce the profit incentive that funds it (often paired with a pharma innovation internal link)
- **PICs** specific to this topic: exclude undocumented immigrants from coverage, exclude specific benefit categories (e.g., elective procedures, reproductive health coverage) to sidestep a religious-liberty or federalism-specific net benefit, keep the employer-based system for some population subset
- **Courts CP** — have the judiciary establish a right to healthcare/insurance rather than Congress legislating it, avoiding the Politics DA link entirely

### Kritik Angles Likely to Come Up
- **Capitalism K** — frames the entire healthcare-insurance system (public or private) as a symptom of commodifying human health under capital; even a "good" Aff plan can be read as reformism that forecloses more radical restructuring
- **Biopower / Foucauldian K** — national health insurance as an expansion of state biopolitical control over bodies and populations (who gets treated, how, under what medical authority)
- **Disability studies K** — critiques the "medical model" embedded in insurance systems (what counts as a treatable condition, who decides), tension between state-administered care and disabled people's autonomy
- **Race/structural racism K** — health outcome disparities are produced by structural racism that a colorblind universal-coverage plan doesn't address, and may even entrench by treating access as the only variable that matters
- **Neoliberalism K** — critiques market-logic solutions (individual mandates, marketplaces, means-testing) as neoliberal governance even when nominally expanding coverage

### How the Aff Answers All of This
- **Vs. DAs**: no-link (the specific plan doesn't trigger the mechanism, e.g. phased implementation avoids shock-based market disruption), link turn (expanding coverage *reduces* downstream costs/ER usage, turning the economy DA), impact turn (some rationing is an acceptable tradeoff against the status quo's rationing-by-price), or uniqueness (the disruption/backlash is already happening or inevitable absent the plan)
- **Vs. States CP**: perm do both (a federal floor + state flexibility); solvency deficits (a patchwork of 50 state systems can't solve uninsured mobility across state lines, employer plans that cross state borders, or a genuinely universal risk pool); "states CP is illegitimate" theory (unpredictable, not real-world, moots the entire resolution every round)
- **Vs. PICs**: perm do the plan minus the PIC's exclusion (if not mutually exclusive); "PICs bad" theory (infinitely regressive, destroys aff ground, not predictable); impact turn the exclusion itself (excluding undocumented immigrants/a benefit category is itself a harm the aff should get to weigh)
- **Vs. Ks**: perm (do the plan and the alt — material policy change alongside a shift in framing/epistemology); alt fails/no solvency (rejecting the aff doesn't materially help anyone currently uninsured); framework (fiat/consequences are a legitimate lens — extinction/large-scale material harm outweighs a purely discursive impact; policymaking education is a Neg impact turn to their framework); link turn (the plan itself resists the K's impact, e.g. universal coverage reduces the state's power to gatekeep care rather than expanding it)
- **Vs. Politics/Spending DA**: uniqueness (health costs are already a fiscal crisis; doing nothing has its own political and budgetary cost), impact turn (short-term political cost is worth it for a popular, tangible policy win), link turn (a popular plan boosts rather than drains political capital)

## Evidence (Cards)
- **Tag** — 1–2 sentence summary of the argument
- **Citation** — author, publication, date (read aloud in round)
- **Body** — excerpt with key lines underlined/highlighted (read at speed)
- Sources: academic papers, government reports, news organizations, think tanks
- **Spreading** — extremely fast delivery to fit more arguments. National circuit standard. Know your judge.

## Flowing
Column-based note system, one column per speech, arguments tracked horizontally across the round. Each off-case argument (DA, CP, K, T) gets its own separate flow sheet. Use shorthand and symbols.

## Judging Paradigms
- **Policymaker** — who has the better policy outcome
- **Stock Issues** — Aff must win all 4 traditional burdens
- **Tabula Rasa** — judge evaluates whatever framework the debaters establish
- **Tech over Truth** — in-round argumentation > real-world accuracy
- **Truth over Tech** — common sense > technical wins

Always ask the judge's paradigm before the round.

## Speaker Points
Typically 26–29 scale. High 28s–29s for exceptional performance.

## Organizations
- **HS**: NSDA (primary), TOC (Tournament of Champions at University of Kentucky — requires qualifying bids at elite invitationals)
- **College**: NDT (National Debate Tournament) + CEDA (Cross Examination Debate Association) — merged topics 1996. Also ADA (American Debate Association).
- College constructives: 9 min. Rebuttals: 6 min. Prep: 10 min/team.
- HS constructives: 8 min. Rebuttals: 5 min. Prep: 8 min/team.

## HS vs College Differences
College topics are broader and more conceptually complex, requiring squad-wide research collaboration. Critical and performative approaches (non-traditional Affs, performance Ks) are significantly more prevalent in college. Kritiks, theory debates, and philosophical argumentation are more advanced. College debaters frequently coach and judge HS programs.
